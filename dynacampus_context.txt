Executive Summary
The existing project is a CCTV-based face detection and recognition system for educational institutions. Its 
implemented baseline uses LFW images from seven identities, 40 x 40 face crops, Haar Cascade 
detection, edge-based preprocessing, PCA-KNN, a small seven-class CNN, an 85% softmax confidence 
threshold for unknown rejection, and desktop/dashboard software. The report records 100% training 
accuracy, approximately 82-86% validation performance, and complete rejection of 400 Olivetti images. 
These results demonstrate a prototype, but not a strong open-set surveillance recognition study. [P0]
The revised project will not train a new output class for every student. It will use a fixed pretrained 
embedding model. New students and faculty will be enrolled by adding one quality-controlled identity 
template to a gallery. The research will address the real domain gap between clean enrollment 
photographs and low-quality CCTV probes, and it will make decisions using multiple frames from a tracked 
video sequence rather than a single image.
The final core method is: SCRFD face detection, five-point face alignment, AdaFace embeddings, a 
lightweight residual CCTV Domain Adapter trained only on development identities, CCTV-aware 
augmented enrollment, quality-aware temporal aggregation, exact cosine gallery matching, and a 
calibrated four-state open-set decision. ArcFace is the baseline. FaceLiVTv2-S is a secondary exploratory 
CNN-Transformer comparison that is added only after the primary AdaFace experiments are complete. 
[R1-R5, R10]
The project will create CampusFace-BD, a consent-based local research dataset with controlled 
enrollment media, two CCTV-style sessions on different days, two camera viewpoints, multiple distances, 
lighting and occlusion conditions, and genuine unknown identities. Public evaluation will use QMUL-
SurvFace as the main surveillance benchmark when access is available, with SCface and RFW as 
supporting evaluations and WIDER FACE only for detector context. [R6-R9]
One-sentence project definition
DynaCampus-FR studies whether training-free dynamic enrollment, CCTV-aware cross-domain adaptation, 
and quality-aware multi-frame aggregation can improve open-set campus face identification under low-
resolution surveillance conditions without retraining the recognition backbone.

DynaCampus-FR: Domain-Adaptive, Quality-Aware Open-Set Face Recognition with Training-Free Dynamic 
Enrollment for Low-Quality Campus CCTV
This is the only primary path. The project is not an attendance application, a mobile application, or a 
generic model-comparison exercise. It is a research study of dynamic open-set identity enrollment and 
recognition across the clean-photo-to-CCTV domain gap.
2.1 Central research problem
Universities have a changing population. A closed-set classifier assigns one output class to every enrolled 
person, so it becomes obsolete when students and faculty join or leave. At the same time, enrollment 
media are usually clean and frontal, while CCTV probes are distant, blurred, compressed, poorly 
illuminated, partially occluded, and temporally inconsistent. The project asks how to recognize enrolled 
identities and reject unknown identities under these conditions without retraining the recognition backbone 
every semester.

3. Research Motivation, Objectives, and Questions
3.1 Revised motivation
Educational institutions increasingly use CCTV, but automatic identification remains unreliable when faces 
are small, blurred, compressed, poorly illuminated, partially occluded, or non-frontal. More importantly, 
most student prototypes assume a fixed identity set and therefore require full retraining whenever the 
population changes. This is incompatible with semester-wise admission, graduation, faculty recruitment, 
and temporary campus visitors.
A practical research system should use an identity-independent embedding model, enroll a new person by 
inserting a template, process several frames from a track, distinguish unknown identities from low-quality 
evidence, and calibrate its operating threshold using open-set biometric metrics. The local research 
challenge is the clean enrollment versus CCTV probe domain shift in a Bangladeshi campus setting.
3.2 General objective
General objective
To develop and experimentally evaluate a domain-adaptive, quality-aware, open-set CCTV face-
identification framework that supports training-free enrollment of new students and faculty under realistic 
low-resolution campus surveillance conditions.
3.3 Specific objectives
7. Create a consent-based, multi-session and multi-camera campus dataset with clean enrollment media, 
CCTV probes, known identities, unknown identities, and condition metadata.
8. Implement a modern pretrained baseline consisting of SCRFD detection, alignment, ArcFace/AdaFace 
embeddings, cosine matching, and dynamic gallery enrollment.
9. Develop CCTV-specific augmentation that reproduces low resolution, motion blur, defocus blur, noise, 
low illumination, compression, pose, and occlusion individually and in combination.
10. Train a lightweight residual Domain Adapter on development identities while freezing the pretrained 
recognition backbone.
11. Develop one-shot and few-shot CCTV-aware enrollment templates that can be inserted without model 
retraining.
12. Develop quality-aware temporal aggregation over tracked face sequences.
13. Calibrate a four-state open-set decision mechanism using score, top-1/top-2 margin, and quality.
14. Evaluate accuracy, open-set errors, robustness, demographic performance where valid, latency, 
memory, and model size.
15. Compare the primary CNN-based AdaFace method with a lightweight hybrid CNN-Transformer model 
after the core method is complete.

6. End-to-End Architecture
Figure 2. Full system architecture with separate enrollment and CCTV paths.
6.1 Enrollment path
22. Receive participant consent and an approved anonymous subject identifier.
23. Capture controlled enrollment photographs and a short enrollment video.
24. Detect faces with SCRFD and reject images with zero or multiple faces unless manually resolved.
25. Align each detected face to the recognizer input format, normally 112 x 112 pixels.
26. Extract normalized AdaFace embeddings.
27. Generate mild, identity-preserving CCTV-style enrollment variants.
28. Estimate quality and combine embeddings into an identity prototype.
29. Insert the prototype and subject ID into the gallery. No recognition backbone retraining occurs.
6.2 CCTV inference path
30. Read a recorded CCTV-style video or permitted live stream.
31. Sample frames at a configured rate; the initial dataset-processing rate is 5 FPS.
32. Detect all faces with SCRFD and retain bounding boxes, landmarks, confidence, and native face size.
33. Associate face detections over time using ByteTrack.
34. Align each usable crop and extract an AdaFace embedding.
35. Apply the residual CCTV Domain Adapter to the probe embedding.
36. Estimate frame quality and aggregate multiple embeddings per completed track.
37. Search the track template against all enrolled prototypes using exact cosine similarity.
38. Use calibrated score, top-1/top-2 margin, and quality thresholds to return one of four states.
7. Face Detection and Alignment
7.1 Detector choice: SCRFD
SCRFD is the fixed detector because it is designed for accuracy under constrained compute and is 
available through the InsightFace ecosystem. The project will begin with a pretrained SCRFD model and 
will not train a detector from scratch. Detector evaluation will still be performed on a manually checked 
local subset. [R1]

7.2 Detector inputs and outputs

    BGR or RGB video frame

7.3 Detection operating rules
 Store detections before resizing so native face size remains available for quality analysis.
 Do not lower the detector threshold only to increase recall without measuring false detections.
 Use one detector configuration for all compared recognition models.
 Report detection recall by face-size category, not only overall recall.
 A detection with severe landmark failure may be retained for detector analysis but excluded from 
recognition.
7.4 Alignment
Apply a similarity transform using the five SCRFD landmarks and the target landmark template expected 
by the recognition model. The result is a normalized face crop. Alignment code must be shared across 
ArcFace, AdaFace, and FaceLiVTv2 unless the official model requires a documented alternative.
Important
Do not apply Canny, Sobel, or other edge extraction before the pretrained recognition model. Use aligned 
RGB/BGR images normalized exactly as required by the model repository.
8. Recognition Backbones
8.1 ArcFace baseline
ArcFace is the strong baseline embedding model. Its additive angular margin objective is widely used to 
produce discriminative face embeddings. The first two-week baseline may use the InsightFace buffalo_l 
package because it combines detection and recognition conveniently. [R2]
8.2 AdaFace primary backbone
AdaFace is the primary backbone. It adapts its training margin according to a sample-quality proxy and 
was designed to improve recognition under quality variation. The project uses a pretrained IR-50 model 
and freezes its weights. [R3]
8.3 FaceLiVTv2-S secondary comparison
FaceLiVTv2 is a 2026 lightweight hybrid CNN-Transformer preprint with an official repository. It is included 
only as a secondary comparison after the complete primary pipeline is stable. It must be described as a 
preprint rather than a peer-reviewed final standard. [R10]

9. Training-Free Dynamic Enrollment
Figure 3. New identities are inserted as templates; the recognizer is not retrained.
9.1 Why the original classifier is infeasible
A softmax classifier has one output neuron per identity. When a new student arrives, the output layer and 
training dataset change. Retraining can be slow, requires earlier data, risks forgetting, and is operationally 
unnecessary because pretrained metric-learning models already support identity-independent 
embeddings.
9.2 Identity prototype
p_i = normalize( sum_k(q_k z_k) / sum_k(q_k) )
z_k is a normalized embedding, q_k is its non-negative quality weight, and p_i is the identity template for subject i.

11. Residual CCTV Domain Adapter
Figure 4. The recognition backbone is frozen; only a small residual adapter is trained.
11.1 Purpose
The adapter learns a small transformation of pretrained embeddings so that clean enrollment samples and 
CCTV probes from the same identity become closer, while different identities remain separated. Test 
identities never participate in adapter training.
11.2 Architecture
Input embedding: 512 dimensions
Layer 1: Linear(512, 256)
Activation: ReLU or GELU
Regularization: Dropout(0.1)
Layer 2: Linear(256, 512)
Residual output: z + adapter(z)
Final step: L2 normalization
z' = normalize(z + g_theta(z))
z is the frozen backbone embedding; g_theta is the trainable residual adapter.

12. Tracking and Quality-Aware Temporal Aggregation
12.1 Why video must be treated as video
A CCTV person appears across many frames. Some frames are sharp, frontal, and large; others are 
blurred, occluded, or tiny. A single-frame decision ignores temporal redundancy and makes recognition 
unstable. ByteTrack will associate detections into track IDs so the system can form one representation per 
track. [R4]
12.2 Frame quality features
Feature Interpretation Initial computation
Detection confidence Detector certainty SCRFD output confidence
Face size Available spatial detail Normalized native face width/height
Sharpness Motion/defocus blur Normalized variance of Laplacian or 
alternative sharpness metric

12.3 Quality score policy
The quality score is a development-set calibration, not a universal truth. Start with normalized features and 
equal or simple weighted averaging, then select weights using development identities only. Store all 
component values so the final paper can explain why a frame was retained or rejected.
q_t = sum_j(w_j f_tj), with w_j >= 0 and sum_j(w_j) = 1
f_tj are normalized quality features for frame t. Weights are selected using the development set.

13. Open-Set Matching and Four-State Decision
13.1 Exact gallery search
For the core research population, stack all gallery prototypes in a matrix and compute the dot product with 
the normalized probe track embedding. This exact search is deterministic, transparent, and fast enough for 
tens or hundreds of identities. FAISS may later be used only for scalability experiments. [R13]

13.3 Four-state rule
if q_track < tau_q:
    return INSUFFICIENT_QUALITY
elif s1 < tau_s:
    return UNKNOWN
elif (s1 - s2) < tau_m:
    return AMBIGUOUS
else:
    return KNOWN(top1_identity)

13.4 Why insufficient quality is separate from unknown
A blurred or tiny face may belong to an enrolled person but contain too little evidence. Labeling it 
“unknown” would confuse identity absence with capture failure. The separate state supports honest 
evaluation and human review.
13.5 Threshold calibration
43. Generate mated and non-mated development searches.
44. Sweep candidate similarity, margin, and quality thresholds.
45. Select operating points that satisfy a target FPIR, initially 1% for the local dataset; report 0.1% only if 
there are enough unknown searches for a stable estimate.
46. Freeze thresholds before final test evaluation.
47. Report the entire trade-off curve, not only the selected operating point.
14. Vision Transformer and RAG Decisions
14.1 Vision Transformer placement
A transformer or hybrid model belongs in the face-embedding stage after detection and alignment. It does 
not replace the dataset, open-set protocol, dynamic gallery, or threshold calibration. FaceLiVTv2-S will use 
the same aligned face crops and evaluation splits as AdaFace so the comparison measures 
representation quality and efficiency rather than pipeline differences. [R10]
Frame -> SCRFD -> alignment -> FaceLiVTv2-S embedding
      -> same gallery/enrollment protocol
      -> same aggregation
      -> same open-set evaluation
14.2 RAG decision
RAG is excluded from the core project because retrieval-augmented generation is intended for retrieving 
external knowledge and generating text, not for producing or calibrating biometric identity embeddings. A 
later incident-summary assistant could retrieve anonymized event logs, but it must remain separate from 
identity decisions and is not part of the four-month core deliverables.

31. Metrics
31.1 Detection metrics
 Precision and recall on the manually annotated local subset.
 Average Precision where the annotation protocol supports it.
 Recall by native face-size group: small, medium, large.
 Miss and false-detection examples by condition.
31.2 Verification metrics
 ROC-AUC and Equal Error Rate.
 TAR at FAR = 1% and FAR = 0.1% when sample size permits.
 FNMR at specified FMR, consistent with biometric reporting conventions. [R15]
31.3 Open-set identification metrics
 Rank-1 and Rank-5 identification rate for mated probes.
 TPIR or 1-FNIR at FPIR = 1%.
 TPIR or 1-FNIR at FPIR = 0.1% only when the number of non-mated searches is adequate.
 Full FNIR versus FPIR curve.
 Unknown rejection rate and false-known rate.
NIST defines FNIR as the proportion of mated searches failing to return the correct identity above 
threshold and FPIR as the proportion of non-mated searches producing one or more candidates above 
threshold. [R15]
31.4 Quality-rejection metrics
 Coverage: fraction of tracks that receive a known/unknown decision rather than insufficient quality.
 Accuracy or FNIR as a function of coverage.
 Error rate before and after the quality gate.
31.5 Efficiency metrics
 Detection latency per frame.
 Recognition and adapter latency per aligned face.
 End-to-end effective FPS.
 Peak CPU RAM and GPU memory.
 Model parameter count and checkpoint size.
 Gallery insertion and exact search time as gallery size increases.
32. Threshold Calibration Procedure
81. Produce development mated searches from enrolled development identities and independent CCTV 
probes.
82. Produce development non-mated searches using identities that are not in the relevant gallery.
83. Save s1, s2, margin, quality, and ground-truth status for every track.
84. Sweep tau_s, tau_m, and tau_q on a grid.
85. Select thresholds based on a predeclared target FPIR and a secondary objective such as minimum 
FNIR.
86. Freeze threshold version T01, T02, and so on; do not silently change it after viewing final test results.
87. Apply the same frozen thresholds to all final test identities for that model/configuration.
No test-set tuning
Any threshold chosen after viewing final test labels invalidates the claimed held-out evaluation. If a threshold 
must change, the final test must be rerun under a newly declared protocol.

47. Limitations That Must Be Stated
 The local dataset population and institution are limited; results may not generalize to all universities or 
demographics.
 Camera placement and recording protocol are controlled compared with unrestricted operational 
CCTV.
 The system uses pretrained models whose original training data and licensing impose constraints.
 The adapter is small and may not solve severe information loss in tiny faces.
 Open-set thresholds depend on gallery size, capture conditions, and target FPIR.
 Quality rejection improves safety by refusing some decisions but reduces coverage.
 Face recognition has privacy, fairness, and governance risks and must not be treated as an
